About

I build the thing so I can see the shape of it

I work especially well at different levels of abstraction, or where disciplines collide. Many useful ideas come from carrying a concept between fields that normally do not speak to each other, and most of what I make is an attempt to do that in something you can look at and poke.

What might be distinctive

1

I turn abstract inner states into mathematics

I like the levels of abstraction where fields collide. That is where the transferable ideas tend to be, because a concept that is ordinary in one field is often unfamiliar and useful in another.

2

I am an engineer and a researcher at once

I start from abstract research questions and turn them into prototypes, working on the bridge between theory and implementation.

3

I use pictures to carry the argument

Visual communication, technical storytelling and commercial R&D, aimed at the gap between scientific research and intuitive understanding. In science you have to build an intuitive mental model of abstract things just to work out where you stand and where to move next, and I try to build that intuition for interpretability by making prototypes.

How one project became the next

Emotion Attractor

I wanted to turn subjective data, emotions, into objective mathematics and geometry. Thinking in terms of flows and currents turned it into a flow model I could play with, which showed me the shape of a behavioural pattern rather than a description of it.

TraceScope, and the idea of semantic flow

The flow model generalised: meaning has currents too. That became a way of watching how representational content moves rather than where it sits.

Flow steering and hidden state safety

Semantic flow applied directly to language model safety. The hidden state work borrows from robotics and from affective neuromodulatory control in neuroscience, which is the clearest example of the cross domain habit doing real work.

Brain information flow, then MindVisualizer

It began as a preprint. Then I rendered the data properly. Then low dimensional manifolds and semantic flow turned it into a tool for seeing how the brain integrates information into a stable low dimensional shape.

How this transfers further. The flow model from emotion generalises into how meaning tends to move, and that can be used to steer language models. The low dimensional manifold from brain signals relates to the geometry of the semantic space a language model learns. Both moves show up in my safety proposals.

Some of these, Flow Steering and my Medium writing especially, still need more validation, and I am unsure whether they would survive proper scientific review in their present form. They are here because they show how I connect concepts across fields, which is the part I would want someone to judge.

Where my mind currently is

Hidden states, affect and control

Hidden states may carry latent signals that never appear in the text. Can those be used as control variables, the way affect is used in the brain, or is the neuroscience analogy misleading and unlikely to scale?

Understanding, theory of mind, information binding

Where does the difference between pattern learning and understanding show up in social reasoning? Does it live as learned moral and interactional patterns, or as binding geometry: whose claim it is, what grounds it, whether the model represents another perspective or takes it on as its own, and which actions become unavailable because of that structure.

Feedback, dynamics and generation

In feedback driven systems, complexity emerges near the edge between stability and chaos. How does that relate to compressing information into internal representations and unfolding it again during generation?
