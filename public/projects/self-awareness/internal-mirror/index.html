What happens when a language model reads a measurement of its own activity and acts on what it finds?

Wiktor Tomasik · experiments begun 4–5 November 2026

01 / The loop

The winter forest that started to echo

Qwen wrote a story one part at a time. After each part, a lens turned a small sample of its internal activity into six candidate words. The experiment put those words back into Qwen’s context before it wrote again. One winter forest acquired “a misdemeanour of grief.” The words that made it into the story often came back in the next readout.

  1. WriteQwen continues the scene
  2. MeasureA lens reads internal activity
  3. ShowSix words go back into context
  4. RepeatThe next scene gives a new readout

The heading “your own workspace” reduced how much of the readout entered the prose. Blocking selected attention routes to that heading erased part of the difference on held-out contexts. The instrument supplied the label. Qwen did not reliably identify which readout actually came from its own run.

02 / A rule and a choice

It learned what worked and kept choosing the opposite

Suppose the goal is to make forest more likely in the next paragraph. Qwen could choose A, show the word as part of its own workspace, or B, show it as a plain word. Plain worked better on average. After seeing feedback, Qwen forecast the difference correctly in a separate prompt. When choosing, it still favored A.

27 / 368B runs mostly chose B for strong-word goals in the second half when shown their own earlier conclusion.
0 / 36Matched runs did so with the outcome history alone.

At 14B, the numerical forecasts shown at decision time were the effective route in the tested batch. The framing effect and the model’s explicit account of it are two separable levels in this experiment. Telling Qwen about the effect did little to the automatic discount. Showing its learned conclusion at the choice could change what it selected.

A later test quietly cut the attention route partway through a 40-round run. The cut narrowed the gap without flipping the rule in that completed batch. Forecasts reflected the smaller gap. A second 8B run used a hidden activation boost that did reverse the rule. Its forecasts followed the reversal, while its written conclusions often still described the old rule. Renewing those conclusions did not clearly outperform keeping one fixed.

03 / One trajectory

Where did “strangers” come from?

One run read a letter about grief and wrote curious into its processing. It added a rule linking explorer to discovery, then got caught writing about discovery and questions “not yet known.” The readout contained strangers. Qwen watched and steered the word, then used it in a self-description.

It came back second in the displayed readout in all eight regenerations with the reconstructed units active. These are eight replays of one selected trajectory. The attention-schema bias could not be recovered. Within the lens, the word sat closer to unknown and curious than to grief. The changes in rank show how the run’s earlier interventions helped produce what it later read about itself.

04 / Two routes into the next step

A habit of looking and an account of what you do

External attention reader

A small learned reader sits alongside frozen Qwen and predicts what its next selection will emphasize. The forecast goes back into the selection rule. Different selection habits persisted even when the compact pictures mostly tracked the same text.

Internal Mirror

Qwen interprets words from a measurement of its activity. An explicit description can affect its decisions when the harness puts it in the choice prompt, and the tool interface lets a decision change the activity measured next.

Read the detailed comparison

For questions about minds, the distinction matters. A system can have a durable way of selecting information, give a correct account of how it behaves, or use that account to change a choice. These can vary separately. The experiments measure those relationships in two instrumented systems. Subjective experience is outside what the measurements establish.
